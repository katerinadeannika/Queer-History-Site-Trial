The Trial That Named a World

1890–1899. Wilde’s trials made letters, rooms, gifts, class, friendship, art, and style appear as public evidence. The trial did not discover a secret world; it forced parts of one into public view.

1895

After Wilde withdrew his criminal-libel case against the Marquess of Queensberry, the evidence developed in that case contributed to Wilde’s prosecution. The first criminal trial did not reach a verdict; the second ended in conviction for gross indecency on 25 May 1895. Wilde and Alfred Taylor were sentenced to two years’ hard labor.

People and evidence

Wilde, Lord Alfred Douglas, Robbie Ross, Constance Wilde, and Havelock Ellis belong to overlapping worlds of literature, friendship, marriage, law, and medical theory. Keep allegation, testimony, conviction, later interpretation, and documented attachment distinct.
