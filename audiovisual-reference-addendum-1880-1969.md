# Audiovisual Reference Addendum
## Queer Worlds, Laws, Figures, and Constellations, 1880–1969

**Working edition · 12 August 2026**

This addendum extends the manuscript’s image references with watch/listen material. It is organized by the manuscript’s chapter structure and is intended as a production-research document, not as a claims source on its own.

## How to use this document

Every chapter has at least:

- **V1 / V2:** two visual sources—film footage, a filmed interview, a documentary segment, or a primary visual record.
- **A1:** one audio source—an interview, oral history, radio programme, spoken-word recording, or historic record.
- **P:** a political/institutional item. These are deliberately included even where the material is hostile: law, medicine, military discipline, censorship, fascism, anti-communism, police practice, or state propaganda often made oppression legible.
- **Q:** a queer-created, pro-queer, or movement-connected voice wherever a usable recording exists.

**Source labels**

- **[Primary]** made in or near the period, or recorded by the person/event itself.
- **[Later testimony]** retrospective interview/oral history.
- **[Context]** documentary or scholarly interpretive source; do not present it as period footage.
- **[Discovery]** a carefully targeted catalogue/search page used when a specific hosted copy moves or has rights restrictions. Replace it with a direct player link if you clear a particular asset.

Do not treat a clip’s upload date as the date of its underlying footage. Confirm creator, date, archive/call number, duration, rights, and whether an uploader has altered audio before using it in a finished production.

# Priority acquisition and clearance list

These are the first assets to verify for a finished site/production package because they meet several goals at once.

1. **Earliest dated Paris and Berlin actuality footage** — establish exact title, year, archive identifier, country of origin, and use conditions.
2. ***Different from the Others* (1919)** — primary queer political film; verify the available cut, language/intertitles, and rights.
3. **Authenticated Vita Sackville-West recording** — capture original catalogue or institutional page, recording date, and recording holder.
4. **Daphne du Maurier filmed interviews** — use the known 1946 and 1971 material only with full programme/upload metadata; label by interview date.
5. **Nazi destruction of Hirschfeld’s Institute / 1933 book-burning footage** — verify event, original reel, and use restrictions; include strong editorial framing.
6. **World War I and World War II footage** — choose specific city and experience, rather than relying on generic battlefield images.
7. **Army–McCarthy hearing footage** — locate a National Archives or other authoritative player with date, witness/speaker, and transcript.
8. **Hays Code primary text plus a visual explainer** — acquire the code’s original text, then use a secondary video only as explanation.
9. **ONE, Mattachine, DOB, Kameny, Stonewall, and Compton’s sources** — favour primary printed matter and participant testimony over retrospective montage.
10. **District Six / Kewpie material** — contact the holding institution before embedding, downloading, or using imagery beyond a linked catalogue reference.

# Editorial and rights reminders

- Link to an institution’s catalogue/player until you have explicit permission to host, reproduce, or excerpt a file.
- For YouTube/Vimeo, keep the uploader, original source, upload date, video title, URL, access date, and rights statement in a separate production log.
- Use captions/transcripts whenever available. Provide a concise description for archival footage that has no usable dialogue.
- Add a content note before footage of persecution, state violence, war, camp liberation, or propaganda.
- Keep **period evidence** separate from **later interpretation** in the page design: a burgundy “Source status” panel can show date, maker, archive, and why the clip is included.
- Political hostile sources belong in the project when they expose a system of power; frame them explicitly and pair them, wherever possible, with queer testimony, organizing, or self-representation.
