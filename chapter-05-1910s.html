War Makes New Rooms

1910–1919. Military discipline, leave, factories, hospitals, letters, Sassoon and Owen, Takarazuka, and wartime intimacy across unequal geographies.

Movement and discipline

War did not liberate queer people. It disciplined bodies, separated people, and made secrecy dangerous. It also moved millions through barracks, hospitals, factories, railways, and ports, creating new conditions for contact and surveillance.

Letters, poetry, performance

Siegfried Sassoon and Wilfred Owen met at Craiglockhart in 1917. Surviving evidence documents friendship, mentorship, and shared literary purpose—not a confirmed romantic partnership. The all-female Takarazuka Revue, founded in 1913, offers a Japanese history of gendered performance and fandom that should not be collapsed into Western identity labels.
